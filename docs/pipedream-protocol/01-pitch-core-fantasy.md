# Pipedream Protocol — GDD
### Section 1 · Elevator Pitch & Core Fantasy

**Tagline:** *“Follow the data. Clean the water. Own the future.”*

---

## 1.1 Elevator Pitch (one paragraph)

**Pipedream Protocol** is a browser-based educational simulation — playable on desktop and mobile — where players become **Pipers**: apprentices in a neon data-center classroom taught by **Brick**, a friendly Lego-style instructor. The player “logs into” a simulated smartphone held in first-person hands, and every action they perform on it — creating an email, posting on social media, checking a bank balance, signing a blockchain transaction, minting an NFT, generating an AI image — materializes instantly as glowing **data-buckets of water** traveling a living network map on the classroom whiteboard. Clean water means private, controlled data; murky or toxic water means surveillance, leakage, and fraud. Across ten mastery ranks (**Novice User → Grandmaster Technician**), players learn email hygiene and privacy defense, watch how banks actually move money (and why fraud costs customers an estimated $80B+ every year), feel exactly how a 30% platform cut drains a creator’s revenue river, and finally discover how decentralized, self-custodied systems reroute power back to the individual — all fully simulated, with zero real accounts, zero real crypto, zero risk.

## 1.2 Core Fantasy

> **“You can see the invisible.”**

The player gains X-ray vision into the internet. Every tap has a visible, beautiful, consequential journey. Nothing about networking, privacy, finance, or crypto remains abstract hand-waving: it is water in pipes, and you are the plumber.

Supporting fantasy beats:
- **Omniscience without effort.** The whiteboard auto-visualizes whatever the player does; learning happens by watching, not by reading manuals.
- **Mastery identity.** Ranks are technical titles (“Protocol Engineer”), not school grades. Leveling up physically expands the world (new network layers bloom onto the map).
- **Cleanliness as power.** Privacy isn’t paranoia — it’s crystal-clear water. Players *feel* contamination as visual pollution and cleanup as instant gratification.
- **Culture, not curriculum.** Meme-aware humor, community leaderboards, cosmetic drip (phone cases, Brick outfits). It should feel like joining a guild, not taking a class.

## 1.3 Design Pillars

| # | Pillar | Rule of thumb |
|---|--------|---------------|
| 1 | **See the Invisible** | Every player action produces a whiteboard visualization within 500 ms. |
| 2 | **Zero-Friction Magic** | Any lesson completable in one thumb-reach session; no typing walls, no menus deeper than 2 levels. |
| 3 | **Same Lesson, Your Language** | All four age cohorts learn identical concepts; only voice, examples, and guardrails adapt. |
| 4 | **Delightful, Never Preachy** | Mistakes spawn fascinating visualizations, never shame. Brick celebrates errors as “great data.” |
| 5 | **Safe Simulation** | No real network calls, no real money, no real keys — ever, in educational mode. |
| 6 | **Community First** | Leaderboards, shared badges, classroom co-op; opt-in and privacy-respecting by design (the game eats its own cooking). |

## 1.4 Target & Format

- **Platform:** Browser (PWA, installable), mobile-first responsive; desktop enhanced.
- **Audience:** 12+ (cohort-adaptive); classrooms, families, self-learners, creator communities.
- **Session shape:** 4–8 minute lessons; a level = 4–6 lessons + 1 capstone “Trace Challenge.” Full campaign ≈ 6–9 hours.
- **Genre:** Guided simulation / interactive visualization / narrative tutorial hybrid.
